# `PctChart` — a scene of scales and the marks drawn on them

**Summary:** A chart you compose in the template: a scene that owns the axes and the colours, and marks inside it — lines and bars — that draw your rows. The data stand beside the picture as a table.
**Entrypoint:** `@pacit/components/chart`
**Selectors:** `pct-chart`, `pct-line`, `pct-bars`, `pct-band`, `pct-axis`, `pct-chart-table`, `pct-chart-zoom`, `pct-chart-legend`, `[pctChartSync]`, `pct-pie`, `pct-gauge`
**Status:** released
**Category:** Data & status
**ARIA APG pattern:** none — the APG has no pattern for a chart. The scene follows the WAI
tutorial on [complex images](https://www.w3.org/WAI/tutorials/images/complex/): a picture
`role="img"` with a name and a description, and the data as a table beside it. Its zoom
buttons follow the APG [toolbar](https://www.w3.org/WAI/ARIA/apg/patterns/toolbar/) pattern.
Named in the class JSDoc.

**A scene and its marks.** `pct-chart` measures its box, holds the scales, the window and the
colours, and draws in layers; `pct-line` and every mark after it is an element inside it whose
drawing the scene stamps into its own `<svg>`
([0099](../decisions/0099-a-chart-is-scales-the-marks-share-and-a-ready-chart-is-a-recipe.md)).
A chart is what its template says it is, and a combination of marks is never a feature
request. The arithmetic underneath — scales, ticks in a zone, the window, the decimation — is
exported, so an application computes beside a chart with what the chart draws with.

**Layers, and the data decimated to the pixel.** The data are one `<svg>`, the axes a second,
each on a compositor layer of its own, so a label or (later) a cursor never repaints sixty
thousand readings; a line keeps the first, lowest, highest and last reading of each column of
pixels, so every peak the eye could see survives and the path holds a few thousand points
([0100](../decisions/0100-a-chart-draws-in-layers-and-its-data-are-decimated-to-the-pixel.md)).

**Axes by unit, colours that stay.** The first unit takes the left axis, the second the
right, the third and fourth stand outside them, and each keeps its place while a series of it
stays; a series takes the palette's next place when it first appears and keeps it, so removing
one repaints none of the others
([0102](../decisions/0102-a-series-owns-its-colour-and-the-colour-is-never-its-only-channel.md)).
The time axis reads the wall clock of `timeZone`: on a day of twenty-five hours it still ticks
00:00, 06:00, 12:00 and 18:00.

**A named picture, and the data as a table.** The drawing is a picture named by `ariaLabel`
and described by `description`; `pct-chart-table` writes the series as the first layer's
`table[pctTable]`, the rows the picture shows, gaps as gaps
([0103](../decisions/0103-a-chart-is-a-named-picture-and-its-cursor-is-the-platforms-range.md)).

**A description of its own, and the picture as a file.** A chart with no `description`
describes the data in the window in words of its own — the stretch, then each shown series
from its lowest to its highest value, its last one, whether it rises, falls or stays level, and
how many stretches it has without readings — and `describe()` reads it out
([0120](../decisions/0120-an-export-is-the-picture-drawn-again-and-a-description-is-the-datas-own-words.md)).
`svg()` writes the picture as a standalone SVG document, its colours computed and written in,
named by the chart's name and carrying its description; `png()` draws that document onto a
canvas at the device's pixel ratio. The cursor, the tooltip, the zoom bar and the legend stay
on the page; the rows go out as CSV through `pct-chart-table`. In print the picture keeps its
colours and the zoom bar is left out.

## Usage

```html
<pct-chart #chart ariaLabel="Supply air, last 24 hours" timeZone="Europe/Warsaw" style="height: 320px">
  <pct-line [data]="readings" x="at" y="value" name="Supply air" unit="°C" curve="step" />
</pct-chart>
<pct-chart-table [chart]="chart" />
```

**Rows, typed.** A mark takes the consumer's rows and reads `x` and `y` off them by a key of
the row whose value is a number — a key of another type does not compile — or by a function of
the row. A value that is not a finite number is a gap. The rows come ordered by `x`; in
development a row out of order is reported, not sorted.

**Colours.** `color` is a palette place from 1 to 8 — `color="3"` is the place too — or any
CSS colour; a number outside the palette is no place, and the series takes the next free one.
A place the template gives wins over a place a series kept, which moves to a free one.

**Units.** `unit` decides the axis: `°C`, `kWh`, `%`, any text; `''` is a plain number;
`boolean` is a yes/no axis from 0 to 1 labelled with `chartOff` and `chartOn`. `pct-axis` fixes
an axis's ends by its unit.

**The cursor.** `cursor` is a model in the horizontal axis's own terms — an instant, or a
number — so a change of the window or of the data leaves it where it is, and two charts can
share one. A mouse's cursor follows it and a press pins it; a tap places it, a tap on its line
clears it, and a finger held still drags it (`cursorMode="tap"` takes presses alone, `none` no
cursor at all). The keys reach it through a range beside the picture, the chart's one stop
([0103](../decisions/0103-a-chart-is-a-named-picture-and-its-cursor-is-the-platforms-range.md)):
every key lands on a reading, a column of pixels at least, and the range's value text is the
reading — the time and each series' value — which a reader announces in its own voice. The
tooltip writes the same reading beside the line; `pctChartTooltip` replaces its rows.

**The window.** `window` is the stretch of the horizontal axis on show; every way of moving it
calls one transform (`pctWindowTransform`), which stops at `limits` — the data's own span when
unset — keeps its span there, and never goes narrower than `minSpan`
([0101](../decisions/0101-the-window-the-cursor-and-the-selection-are-the-applications-models.md)).
A mouse's drag across the plot pans it and Ctrl with the wheel zooms about the pointer (the
wheel alone scrolls the page); a finger dragged across pans, one dragged down the page is the
page's, two fingers pinch about their middle, and a double tap returns to `defaultWindow`. The
keys `+` and `−` zoom about the cursor, Shift with an arrow pans a tenth, `0` returns. The zoom
bar over the plot's corner does all of it with single presses — the twin a pinch and a drag
owe (WCAG 2.5.1, 2.5.7); `zoomButtons` off moves it elsewhere, as `pct-chart-zoom`. `follow`
keeps the window's end on `now()` while it touches it. A reader hears the window's new range
once it rests. `zoom="none"` keeps the window where the application puts it.

**The second channel.** Series N has a colour, a dash pattern and a marker shape
([0102](../decisions/0102-a-series-owns-its-colour-and-the-colour-is-never-its-only-channel.md)):
a mark draws all three as its swatch, which the legend, the tooltip and the markers on the
cursor's line stamp. The lines draw the dash under forced colours, where every colour is one,
and always with `distinct="all"`.

**Points and bubbles.** `pct-points` draws a mark per row at its `x` and `y`, the rows in any
order and a row with no finite value no point ([0105](../decisions/0105-a-point-is-picked-by-distance-and-the-y-window-is-the-first-axiss.md)): the series' marker shape in
its colour, `radius` wide, `hollow` as an outline. With `size` read off the rows the marks are
bubbles whose AREA grows with it — the largest `maxRadius` wide, none under two pixels — and
the size is a column of the table and a row of the tooltip, named by `sizeName` and written
with `sizeUnit`. Two points closer than two pixels draw one mark, so fifty thousand rows stay
a light picture. The cursor stands on the nearest point within sixteen pixels of the pointer —
on a scatter it is always a point, on a line with points the line's reading until a point is
near — and a press on a point emits `pick` with its row. `xUnit` writes the unit of a `linear`
horizontal axis at its end and after the position the cursor reads. With the chart's `animate` a
point's move and a bubble's size ease from what was drawn; points added or dropped are drawn at
once, and the table's size column reads the rows.

**A zoom on both axes.** `zoom="xy"` makes every move of the window work the first value axis
too, through `yWindow`, a model in that axis's unit: the same transform run in the axis's own
pixels, let go to `null` once it covers the data's domain again; the other axes follow the same
stretch of pixels through their own scales, so the series keep their places over one another.
Shift with an arrow up or down pans the first axis a tenth; `0` and a double tap return both.

**A logarithmic axis.** `pct-axis scale="log"` maps its unit by the logarithm: whole decades
from the one under the lowest value to the one over the highest, each the same height, ticked
1, 2 and 5 of every decade while they fit and by decade when they do not. A value at or below
zero has no logarithm — it is a gap, reported in development — and with no positive value in
sight the axis shows the three decades under its top; `floor` and `ceiling` hold above zero.

**The legend.** `pct-chart-legend` lists the series beside the chart: each entry a button named
by the series, pressed while it is shown, that writes the chart's `hiddenSeries`; its swatch,
its unit where the chart holds more than one, and a value — the reading under the cursor, or
what `value` asks for with none: the latest reading in the window, their sum or their mean.
`removable` adds a button per entry that emits `remove`; dropping the mark is the
application's. `pctLegendItem` replaces the row.

**Charts in step.** `pctChartSync` on a common ancestor shares the window and the cursor of the
charts inside it, and gives their plots the same edges, so a series over a band of states lines
up whatever their axes.

**A brush and a navigator.** `brush` turns a drag across the plot into `selected` instead of a
pan ([0119](../decisions/0119-a-brush-is-the-selection-drawn-by-a-drag-and-moved-by-two-ranges.md)):
a drag inside the selection moves it whole, one on an edge moves that edge, and a tap outside
lets it go. Each edge has a grip on the plot and a range beside the picture — two more stops,
read like the cursor's, their arrows a hundredth of the window and their page keys a tenth.
Enter on an edge, a double tap inside the selection or `zoomToSelection()` shows it as the
window; Escape lets it go. A tap on a grip picks the edge and the next tap puts it down: the
single-pointer twin of the drag (WCAG 2.5.7). Bound with `[(selected)]` to another chart's
`window`, a low chart with `brush`, `zoom="none"` and `cursorMode="none"` is its navigator.

**Fetching as the window moves** is the application's: when and how much, and how long after
the last move. A recipe on the models and the transform:

```ts
// Fetch a margin around the window, after it has rested for 250 ms.
effect((onCleanup) => {
  const w = chart.window() ?? chart.dataSpan();
  if (!w) return;
  const margin = (w.to - w.from) / 2;
  const timer = setTimeout(() => load(w.from - margin, w.to + margin), 250);
  onCleanup(() => clearTimeout(timer));
});
```

**Live data, and a recount eased.** A reading added at the end and the oldest dropped costs the
reading added: a mark keeps the rows it read, and when the next `data` holds the same row
objects with some gone from the front and some new at the back — what
`rows => [...rows.slice(1), row]` gives — it reads only the new ones
([0121](../decisions/0121-live-data-is-read-where-it-changed.md)). Eight series of 60 000
readings under `follow`, each taking a reading ten times a second, cost 9 to 13 ms a tick in
the three engines. A row kept is not read again, so a corrected row is a new object; an
accessor that takes the row's index reads every row every time. `animate` eases a change that
only moves the values — the same positions, in the same number — over twice the motion axis's
transition; readings added are drawn at once, so a live line never slides, and
`prefers-reduced-motion` draws every change at once. The path, its axis and the value the
cursor reads move with it; the table and the description read the rows. Points, bubbles, ranges
and candles ease with it, and `pct-pie` and `pct-gauge` take an `animate` of their own.

**Bars.** `pct-bars` draws a bar per band of the scene: a calendar interval of `timeZone`
when the chart has an `interval` — a whole hour, day, week, month or year, twenty-five hours
long on the day the clocks go back — or a category of a `band` axis (`categories`, a row's `x`
the category's index). The last row that falls in a band stands; a band with none has no bar
and a zero is a line. Marks of one `group` stand side by side in a band, marks of one `stack`
on each other, positives up and negatives down from zero (`pctStack`). `compare` lays a second
period beside the first by position, the i-th row to the i-th band, lighter and named by
`compareName`: February next to March is twenty-eight bars beside thirty-one, and the tooltip
gains a note under the rows — the difference between the bar and the compared one, in percent
of the compared (`chartDifference`, `+12%`), which the range's value text reads too; a mark
writes such notes through `notesAt`. `labels` writes the values over the bars, thinned where
they would touch; the selected band's label is never thinned away and is the bold one. A press
on a bar emits `pick` and selects its band, a second press lets it go; `selected` is a model,
drawn across the plot; Enter and Space select at the cursor. The table gives such a chart a row per band, and `summary` a foot with each column's
sum or mean.
`orientation="horizontal"` turns the chart: the x axis runs down the left side and the value
axes along the bottom and the top, the bars grow to the right with their labels beside their
ends, the cursor's line lies across with the tooltip under it, a drag up and down pans and the
page keeps the horizontal gesture; a `pct-line` on such a scene runs down too.

**Annotations.** Four marks bring no series — no axis, no column in the table, no legend
entry unless `legend` asks for one — and are told apart by a `label` and a `color` (a palette
place or a colour of the consumer's own, the skin's own otherwise). `pct-reference` draws a
line across the plot at a value of a `unit` (`y`) or at a position (`x`), `dash` dashes it, and
its label stands at the line's end; `pct-span` lays a band between two positions or, with
`axis="y"`, between two values, under the series, its label in the corner; `pct-note` sets a
text in a box on a point, with a leader to it and a dot on it, over the series — the scene
places every note together so that none covers another and every one stays on the plot
(`pctPlaceNotes`), `position` asks for a side and `auto` takes the first with room; `pct-now`
is the line of the present moment, moving with the clock once a second while it is in the
window, or pinned to `at`. All four turn with the scene. A reader sees none of them, so each
adds a sentence to the picture's description after the consumer's own — `Comfort 21 °C.`,
`Night tariff 25 October 2026 00:00 – 25 October 2026 06:00.`, `Window opened (25 October
2026 09:00, 21.9 °C).`, `Report taken 25 October 2026 16:00.` — and the legend's toggle on an
annotation asked to stand there hides it, picture and sentence alike (`hiddenSeries`, by its
`id` or label). The layer a mark draws in is its own to say (`PctMark.layer`: `back`, `data`
or `front`), and so are its sentence (`describe`), its note boxes and its legend entries;
`PctAnnotation` is the base the four share. **A line cut by its thresholds.** `threshold` on
`pct-line` names the values the line changes its colour at: from each `at` up it is drawn in
that `color` until the next, under the first in its own. The path is drawn once per colour and
clipped to the band of values, so a reading on a threshold is cut there and nothing is
interpolated; the swatch keeps the series' own colour.

**A band of states.** `pct-band` draws what a device was doing through the day — a heat pump
heating, defrosting, standing by — as a block per segment of its `data`, each read by `from`,
`to` and `state`, in the colour of its state (`states`: a palette place or a colour, and a
label, per key; a state given none takes the next free place and is written by its key). It
brings no series and no value axis: by default it draws in a **lane** of its own under the
plot, a strip of the `band-height` token the scene lends it
([0104](../decisions/0104-a-state-band-draws-in-a-lane-the-scene-lends-and-alone-takes-the-plot.md));
`lane="plot"` tints the whole plot behind the series instead, and bands that name the same
lane share it. A chart whose marks declare no series lends the lanes the whole plot, so
`pct-chart` holding one `pct-band` is a band of states with a time axis, that `pctChartSync`
keeps in step with the chart above it. The tooltip names the state under the cursor, with its
swatch, and how long it held — `Heating · 2 hr, 15 min`, by `Intl.DurationFormat`, which the
three engines have; the legend lists the states, each with a toggle that hides its segments,
and shows the duration beside the state under the cursor; the data table gets a column of
text with a row per segment, exported as text; the keys step from segment to segment as they
step from reading to reading; a reader hears how long each state held in the window (`Mode:
Heating 8 hr; Off 9 hr.`). Under forced colours the segments are boxes of one colour, and the
words tell the states apart. Daily hours of operation are a recipe, not a mark: sum a state's
segments per day into rows for `pct-bars` with `interval="day"`.

**A step and what it holds.** `curve="step"` holds each reading until the next, which is right
for data written when they change; `end` carries the last reading to a moment, `now` for a
series whose last value is still the value.

**A monotone curve.** `curve="monotone"` joins the readings by the cubic of Fritsch and
Carlson ([0106](../decisions/0106-an-area-stands-on-its-stack-and-a-range-is-two-values-at-a-time.md)):
between two readings the line stays between their values and never overshoots, and the
tooltip reads the same curve (`pctMonotoneTangents`, `pctHermite`). It is a curve of a line, an
area and a range alike; `smooth` is no name here, because it promises nothing.

**Areas and stacks.** `pct-area` draws a series as an area: alone from zero to its values, or
with `stack` from the top of the areas naming the same stack before it, so a sum reads as one
shape and each part as a layer of it. The scene computes each stack once (`areaStackOf`): at
every position any member has a reading at, every member read as it is drawn, so series on
different clocks still add up; a gap in a member is a gap in its own layer and a zero for the
ones above it, and a hidden series leaves the stack. The tooltip and the table read each
series' own value, and the marker on the cursor's line stands on the drawing (`drawnAt`).
`normalize` draws the stack as shares: every value its share of the position's total in
percent, the unit `%`, the picture a hundred tall, a total of nothing a gap, and each row reads
the share with the value in brackets — `40% (2 kW)`.

**A histogram** is a recipe: `pctBin` sorts values into bins — by Sturges' rule, by Freedman
and Diaconis, by a count or by the edges given — on a nice step with the edges on the step and
the last bin closed, and the bins are the `categories` of a band axis with `pct-bars` counting
each.

**Ranges and candles.** `pct-range` reads a `low` and a `high` off each row and draws the band
between them, a gap in either a gap in both; the series' value is the high, which the axis and
the marker follow, and its text is both — `3 °C – 11 °C` — in the tooltip, in the range's value
text and in the table's cell (a mark may write a row itself: `readAt`). With `open` and `close`
too the band is candles: a wick from the low to the high and a body from the open to the
close, held until the next, in the skin's rise colour (`body-bg-rise`) where the close stands at or above
the open and its fall colour (`body-bg-fall`) under; the close is the value, the open, the high and the low are
lines of the tooltip and columns of the table, and the table has a row per candle. Under forced
colours a rising body is hollow and a falling one filled. With the chart's `animate` the band,
the wicks and the bodies ease from what was drawn, and the value the cursor reads with them; the
table reads the rows.

**Boxes.** `pct-box` reads the `values` of each row and draws their distribution
(`pctQuantiles`): the quartiles as the box, the median across it, whiskers to the farthest
values within one and a half interquartile ranges, and every value past them as a point. The
median is the value; the low, the quartiles, the high and the outliers are lines of the
tooltip and columns of the table, the outliers listed six at a time with a count of the rest.
Candles and boxes take six tenths of the smallest spacing between their positions — a body
from 2 to 24 pixels wide, a box from 4 to 40 — and the axis holds the outliers.

**A heatmap.** `pct-heat` reads a position `x`, a row `y` and a `value` off each row of the
data and draws a cell per band and row
([0113](../decisions/0113-a-heatmap-is-rows-the-scene-lays-and-a-colour-the-stylesheet-mixes.md)):
the bands are the chart's — an `interval` of time or the categories of a band axis — and the
rows are the names `y` gives, in the order of `rowOrder` or of their first appearance, laid
across the plot by the scene and named at its side (under it, turned). Each cell is painted on
the skin's ramp — `heat-bg-1` to `heat-bg-8`, or with `scale="diverging"` from `heat-bg-low`
through `heat-bg-mid` to `heat-bg-high` — by the stylesheet mixing the two steps its value
falls between (`pctRampStop`, `color-mix`), so a theme repaints it and the server draws it
without measuring a colour; under forced colours a cell past the middle is filled and one
before it outlined. The ramp runs over the data's extent, a diverging one centred on zero and
as far on both sides as the farther value, or over a `domain` given. Every row is a series —
with no axis and no palette place — so the legend lists it, the tooltip reads it at the
cursor with the marker at the row's middle, and the table carries it as a column; a value
that is not a finite number is a gap drawn as no cell. `labels` writes each value on a cell
wide enough, in dark text on the pale steps and light on the deep; a press on a cell emits
`pick` with its row. `pct-chart-ramp` beside the chart shows the ramp of each heatmap with the
domain's ends — the middle too, diverging — in the chart's locale and unit, hidden from a
reader since the description says the same reach in words.

**A pie.** `pct-pie` is a scene of its own and needs no `pct-chart`
([0115](../decisions/0115-a-pie-is-a-scene-of-shares-and-a-gauge-is-a-meter-drawn-as-an-arc.md)):
it reads a `value` and a `name` off each row of the data and draws a slice per row with its
share of the turn, from twelve o'clock or from `startAngle`, each slice a series — placed in
the palette by first appearance, painted by its place like a bar, with the pie's `unit` — so
the legend lists it, the table carries it as a row with its value and its share, and a
`summary="sum"` foots the total. `donut` hollows the pie by that share of its radius, held at
nine tenths; the smallest values past `others` (six) fold into one slice of the rest, named by
`chartOthers`, that holds their sum — never one alone. `labels="auto"` writes each slice's
name and share beside the arc on a leader from its middle, the right half laid top to bottom
and the left the same, a label pushed down a line where it would cover the one before and
dropped where it would leave the picture; the circle leaves room for the widest. The picture
is a named image with a description generated from the shares when the consumer gives none,
the cursor's range its one stop (0103): the arrows move from slice to slice, `Home` and `End`
to the first and last, `Enter` and `Space` select the slice under the cursor and emit `pick`,
`Escape` clears it, and the range reads `Name: value, share`. A hover reads a slice in a
tooltip with its value and its share; a press picks it; a `selected` slice stands
`pie-size-selected` out along its bisector. Under forced colours each place is a hatch of
its own (`PCT_SERIES_HATCHES`, eight patterns in the pie's `<defs>`), on the slices and the
swatches alike, and the gap between slices is a stroke of `pie-gap` in `pie-border`.
`animate`, off by default, eases a change of the values over twice the motion axis's
transition while the same slices stand in the same order; a slice added, dropped or hidden
is drawn at once, and the range, the table and the description read the values (0121).

**A gauge.** `pct-gauge` is a meter drawn as an arc (0115): a `div role="meter"` with
`aria-valuemin`, `aria-valuemax`, `aria-valuenow` and a value text of the value in its unit
and the word of the band it falls in — `21.5 °C, Comfort` — the drawing hidden from a
reader. `bands` are `PctGaugeBand`s, `from` inclusive to `to` exclusive and the last closed,
each in a `tone` of the semantic tier (`gauge-bg-success` and its three siblings) with a
`label`; the host carries `data-pct-tone` of the band the value is in, so a skin, a reader
and a page with no colours all get the state (0076). The track spans `sweep` degrees (240)
about the bottom, the bands lie on its rim, the value fills the track from the minimum in
the band's tone — or `gauge-fg` outside any band — or, with `indicator="needle"`, a needle
points at it; the value and the band's word stand in the middle, the ends are labelled, and
a `null` value reads `No reading` at the minimum. The arc's width is `gauge-width`, and the
gauge measures itself like a chart, by a `ResizeObserver`, drawing a skeleton on the server.
`animate`, off by default, eases the fill or the needle from the value drawn to the new one; a
value that comes or goes is drawn at once, and the meter's value and its words are the new
value from the start (0121).

## Known limitations

- **An export carries the fonts by name.** The SVG names the scene's font family and does not
  embed it, so a viewer without the font draws the text in its own; the PNG is drawn by the
  browser that has it. A trend is the least-squares slope over the window, called level when
  it moves less than a tenth of the series' spread.
- **A brush selects along the horizontal axis alone.** A selection is a stretch of time or
  position, never a box of values; its grips are drawings, and the target is the plot within
  12px of an edge, so two edges closer than that are told apart by the side the press is on.

- **A pie draws `others` slices and one of the rest.** A slice's label is dropped where it
  would leave the picture, so a pie of many small shares reads its names in the legend and
  the table; a slice is picked by its series, and two rows of one name are one series.
- **A gauge's meter is ARIA on a `div`.** The platform's `<meter>` draws its own bar and has
  no value text, so the role is written; the bands are the application's, and a value outside
  every band is drawn in `gauge-fg` with no word.
- **A log axis holds lines and points.** Bars and areas stand on zero, which a log axis has
  not, so a series of bars on a `log` axis is not drawn; the first value axis alone takes the
  `yWindow`, the others follow it in pixels.
- **The cursor's keys move it, not the window.** A cursor outside the window stays where it
  was put and draws nothing until the window comes back to it.
- **A lane takes its height from the plot.** The chart's height is the consumer's CSS, so a
  band under the plot leaves the series a shorter plot rather than a taller chart; a chart
  of a band alone needs the height of its lane and the time's labels, about 72px.
- **The moment of now ticks once a second, in the browser alone.** On the server the line
  stands where the page was rendered; a note's box is measured with the scene's font, not
  drawn and read back, so a text in a wider font than the axes' overruns its box.
- **Where several rows fall in one band, the last one stands.** A series summed by the hour
  is the application's to sum; the bar draws what the row says.
- **The dash patterns and the marker shapes are not tokens.** A dash is a `strokeStyle` in
  DTCG, a type the design tool's bridge does not read, so both tables stand in the stylesheets
  and the contract (`PCT_SERIES_MARKERS`), eight each, and a ninth series starts over.
- **The rows must come ordered by `x`.** A row out of order is reported in development and
  drawn where it stands.
- **A stacked area is not decimated.** Its top is a sum that the decimation of one series
  cannot keep, so a stack is drawn at every position of its members in the window; the
  members of one stack should share a `curve`, since each is read by its own.
- **The heatmap's ramp is eight steps, mixed.** Two values a seventh of the ramp apart can
  take the same two steps in different shares, which the eye reads as a continuum; a
  consumer who needs a step function sets `domain` and reads the table. `color-mix()` needs
  a 2023 browser, and an older one draws the nearer step.
- **The monotone curve agrees with its reading on linear axes.** On a logarithmic axis the
  drawn curve bends in the logarithm and the reading is the data's.
- **A live series shown whole stays dear.** With all 60 000 readings of eight series in the
  window, a reading each a tenth of a second still costs 50 to 66 ms a tick: the decimation of
  the whole series to the pixel, not the reading. Readings gathered and added once a second cost
  a tenth of it.
- **`animate` moves what keeps its shape.** Lines, areas, bars, points, ranges, the pie and
  the gauge ease a recount; a compared period's bars, the boxes and the heatmap draw every
  change at once, and so does every mark when readings, slices or a value come or go.
- **The width follows the container, the height is the consumer's** — `16 / 9` of the width
  when neither height nor aspect ratio is set.
- **Labels are measured with a canvas in the axis's font.** A skin that sets
  `font-variant-numeric` on an axis changes widths the canvas does not see.
